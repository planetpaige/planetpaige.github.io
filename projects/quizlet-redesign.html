Quizlet Redesign

A UX research and design project for IN4MATX 131 exploring how UCI STEM students study with Quizlet. Through interviews, personas, and wireframes, our team designed a student verification layer that helps learners find accurate study sets faster and with more confidence.

Role

  1. Phase 0

    Understanding Users

    Goal: Learn how UCI STEM undergraduate students use Quizlet and other study tools before introducing changes.

    What I did: As a team, we discussed and developed our interview protocol to focus on current use cases of the software. I functioned as one of the primary UX researchers and helped run semi-structured interviews. After all the interviews were conducted, we came back together as a team to analyze the data through affinity mapping and AI-assisted analysis, then built a proto-persona, Sophia.

    What we found:

    • Students use Quizlet primarily as a pre-exam cram session.
    • They rely on publicly accessible sets, rather than creating their own.
    • The interface is well liked, but the recent incorporation of a paywall on advanced features is a common frustration.
    • Some participants feel less efficient studying without it.

    We used these observations to come up with a solution focused on improving time efficiency.

  2. Phase 1

    Problem Statement

    Goal: Turn our research into a single focused problem worth redesigning.
    What I did: I led the user story and task flows. Interviews showed student rarely make their own sets and often get frustrated by inaccurate information on public ones, so I framed the story around a study looking for trustworthy material. I mapped the current flow (search, then manually checking each set) against our redesigned flow centered around a "Student Verified" filter.
    Key Decisions:

    • Choose a small, achievable scope instead of a complete app overhaul
    • Simplified the task flow
    • Added an optional step where users can publically tag sets as "Student Verified."
  3. Phase 2

    Early Mockups

    Goal: Turn the redesigned flow into screen that fit naturally with Quizlet's existing interface.
    What I did: I led the wireframing with a teammate who focused on the Low-Fidelity sketch. After deciding what direction we were going in, I built up the mid-fidelity wireframes in Figma.
    The soultion:

    • A "Student Verified" filter to the search results page
    • Added a "Student Verified" badge next to each flashcard set to show how many times it's been verified
    • Kept the rest of the layout the same as the exisiting site to create a usable base for my teammates.

    What I learned:

    • Inconsistent layer names slowed us down, so I would set naming conventions from the start.
    • We needed to be careful not to add too many new features or change the layout too much, as this could confuse users.
    View Mid-Fidelity Mockup
  4. Phase 3

    Bringing it all together

    COMPLETE

    My teammates then built high-fidelity mockups and an interactive prototype to include hover behavior for the filter menu. This went on to be the version we presented to our classmates by the end of the quarter.
    What I learned: I learned how to work with a team of designers to create a cohesive design. I also learned how to use Figma to create high-fidelity mockups and an interactive prototype. Finally, I learned how to present our work to an audience and answer questions about our design decisions.
